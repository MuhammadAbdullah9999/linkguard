A message arrives from an unknown number: a recruiter has seen your profile, the job is part-time, and you can earn good money for a few minutes of work each day. This is one of the fastest-growing scams on messaging apps.

## How the task scam works

1. **The hook.** An unsolicited message offers flexible work, usually liking videos, boosting product ratings or "optimising" apps.
2. **Small early payments.** You complete a few simple tasks and may even receive a small payout, which builds trust.
3. **The deposit.** You are told to pay in your own money, often in cryptocurrency, to unlock "premium tasks" with bigger commissions.
4. **The trap.** Each level asks for more. Withdrawals never arrive, or require a further fee.

## Red flags

- You did not apply, and a stranger found you through a wrong number or "your resume".
- High pay for very little skill or time.
- Contact moves quickly to WhatsApp or Telegram.
- You are asked to pay to earn.
- Payment in crypto or by transfer to a personal account.
- A link to a polished "work platform" that you have never heard of.

## What real employers do

Real employers do not ask you to pay to start work, do not recruit by random text, and do not pay for liking videos. They have a company website, interviews and contracts.

## What to do

1. Do not reply, and do not click the link.
2. Block and report the number in the app.
3. Forward SMS to 7726 (SPAM) where available.
4. If you shared personal details, watch your accounts and consider a credit freeze.
5. If you paid, contact your bank or exchange and file a report with your national fraud agency.

## Check the platform link

If a link claims to lead to a job platform, scan it first. LinkGuard looks at domain age, certificates, reputation and look-alike tricks, which can quickly show that a "company" site is brand new and unknown.
