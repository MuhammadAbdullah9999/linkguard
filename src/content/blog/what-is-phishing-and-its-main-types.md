Phishing is when someone pretends to be a trusted company or person to trick you into giving up information or money. The word is a play on "fishing". The message is the bait.

## The main types

### Phishing (email)
A message that looks like it comes from your bank, a delivery company or an online service. It contains a link or attachment and urges you to act.

### Smishing (SMS and messaging apps)
The same trick by text, WhatsApp or other chat apps. Fake delivery notices and toll messages are classic examples.

### Vishing (voice)
Phone calls from someone claiming to be your bank, tax authority or tech support. Scammers may spoof the caller ID and speak with confidence.

### Quishing (QR codes)
A QR code that leads to a fake page. Often placed as stickers on parking meters or inside emails.

### Spear phishing
A targeted message that uses details about you or your employer, such as your name, boss or recent purchase.

### Clone phishing
A copy of a real message you already received, with the link or attachment swapped for a malicious one.

## What they want

- Passwords and one-time codes
- Card numbers and bank details
- Personal data for identity theft
- Money sent directly, often by gift card, wire or crypto
- Installation of malware

## Common tactics

- **Urgency:** "within 24 hours"
- **Fear:** "your account is locked"
- **Greed:** "you have won"
- **Authority:** a bank, a government agency, your boss

## The one habit that defeats most phishing

**Do not use the link or number in the message.** Go to the real website or app yourself, or call the number printed on your card or on the company's official site. This single habit stops the majority of attacks.

## Other good habits

- Use unique passwords and a password manager.
- Turn on two-factor authentication, ideally with an app rather than SMS.
- Keep your phone and apps updated.
- Scan unfamiliar links before you tap them.
